OBJECTIVE AND METHOD
The requested objective was repeated agent review, scoring, improvement and
before/after comparison until accuracy, readability, clarity and formatting each
reached at least 9/10, with a final independent review.

Three section agents reread the original README. Each initially owned a section:
1. Purpose, scope and navigation.
2. Verified figures and interpretation.
3. Coverage, provenance, gaps, reproduction and validation.
Ownership rotated in rounds 2 and 3. Every agent therefore reviewed every section,
compared it with the original, proposed or challenged improvements, and scored the
revised section. A separate fourth agent performs the final independent review.

This is a rubric-based editorial A/B comparison, not an empirical user experiment.
There was no random assignment, blinded reader panel or measured reading-time
study. Scores are judgments tied to recorded defects and checks, not statistical
estimates. Reviewers sometimes scored the same original section differently;
scorecard.csv preserves their individual ratings rather than hiding disagreement.

RUBRIC
Accuracy: source-backed claims, paths, counts, dates, scope and reproducibility.
Readability: understandable prose, useful ordering and manageable density.
Clarity: unambiguous definitions, caveats and actions for the reader.
Formatting: consistent plain-text headings, spacing, lists and path presentation.
A score of 9 allows minor optional polish but no material defect. A factual defect
blocks accuracy of 9. The complete rubric and protocol are in rubric.txt.

WHAT CHANGED
Round 1 rebuilt the stale collection-stage overview around the current analysis.
It added routes to 29 cleaned CSV files, annual institution/degree distributions,
two executed notebooks and the PAL event matrix. It replaced universal manifest
and validation claims with records scoped to each collection stage.

Round 2 challenged each section with a different reviewer. Repairs included an
inventory link, acronym and file-opening guidance, exact heading references,
removal of a blanket comparability claim, and precision about degree-granting
public-DLI graduate exemptions. Reproduction now distinguishes inspected command
order from evidence of prior successful notebook runs.

Round 3 rotated again. It corrected a substantive geography error: destination-
based year-end stocks and DLI institution-province tables are different. It also
clarified stocks, nonadditive headcounts, remaining access failures, and which
notebook each saved execution count describes.

Scores did not rise automatically. For example, a rotated reviewer rated the
round 2 interpretation section 8 for accuracy and 8 for clarity because of geographic
scope. That defect was repaired before the section was rated 9 on both measures.
Some already-strong sections kept their scores after small refinements.

RESULTS BEFORE FINAL INDEPENDENT REVIEW
All three sections received 9/10 on each metric in their third-round reviews.
Nine review records document the complete three-agent by three-section rotation.
See scorecard.csv and *_round1.txt, *_round2.txt, *_round3.txt for explanations.

The original has 1,574 words and the third-round candidate 1,733 words. The modest
increase adds current coverage and essential qualifications. Improvement is not

LIMITATIONS
The README review did not rerun the analysis pipelines or refresh online sources.
It inspected scripts, saved results and source records. Prior execution receipts
are correctly labelled prior evidence. The README can clarify missing PAL data,
but cannot remove the underlying absence of matched university recipient records.
