README review protocol
Target: collection top-level README.txt. Supporting READMEs are evidence, not targets.
Original: original_README.txt. Preserve each integrated candidate for comparison.

Each section and the whole document receive four independent 0-10 scores:
Accuracy: statements, counts, scope, source ownership, paths and commands match files.
Readability: plain language, manageable length, useful ordering and no compressed text.
Clarity: unambiguous measures, dates, limitations and next actions; no assumed knowledge.
Formatting: consistent plain-text headings, spacing, lists, paths and scanability.
9 means no material defect and only minor optional polish. 10 means no identified
improvement within scope. 7-8 means useful but concrete repair needed; <=6 means
material confusion, inaccuracy or usability failure. Scores are editorial judgments,
not empirical reader-test measurements. A factual defect blocks accuracy >=9.

Round 1: each assigned agent reads the entire original, evaluates its section,
proposes fixes, edits only its owned section file, then scores original vs candidate.
Round 2: rotate sections among the three agents; compare original vs integrated
candidate, identify regressions, improve each assigned section, and rescore.
Round 3: rotate again so every agent has reviewed every section. Challenge the
latest section against the original, repair defects, and score all four metrics.
Every section must reach >=9. Additional rounds are required for lower scores.
Final: a separate agent reviews original vs final whole README and the review
report. Verify all sections, evidence and scores; revise if any metric <9.
Root integrates files, checks local paths and factual anchors, and records changes.
Do not force scores upward or hide disagreement. No changes to research data.
